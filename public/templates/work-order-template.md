## Included fields

- **Work Order ID** — A unique reference for communication and history.
- **Priority and status** — How urgent the work is and where it sits in the workflow.
- **Requester and location** — Who reported the issue and where the technician should go.
- **Asset and problem** — The affected equipment and the original symptoms.
- **Assignment and schedule** — The responsible technician and promised work date.
- **Close-out fields** — Completion date, labor, parts, and the permanent repair note.

## How to use it

1. **Capture the symptom before the diagnosis** — Keep the requester's original description, then let the technician add cause and repair details during close-out.
2. **Use a small status set** — Requested, approved, assigned, in progress, on hold, completed, and closed are enough for most teams and make backlog reporting consistent.
3. **Close with useful history** — Record the work performed, parts, labor, and follow-up need. That close-out becomes the starting point for the next repair on the same asset.

## FAQ

### What should a maintenance work order include?

A work order should include a unique ID, requester, priority, status, location, asset, problem description, assignee, schedule, labor, parts, completion date, and close-out notes.

### What is the difference between a work request and a work order?

A request reports a need. A work order is the approved, assigned, and trackable record used to plan and complete that work.

### Can I download this work order template in Word?
