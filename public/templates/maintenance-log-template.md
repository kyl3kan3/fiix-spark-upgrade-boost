## What's in the template

1. **Date**: The date the maintenance work was performed.
2. **Work Order ID**: The reference number of the related work order.
3. **Asset ID**: The unique identifier for the equipment or asset.
4. **Asset Name**: A plain-language name for the asset.
5. **Location**: Where the asset is installed, such as a building, floor or room.
6. **Maintenance Type**: The category of work, such as Preventive or Corrective.
7. **Description**: A short summary of the work performed.
8. **Technician**: The person who completed the work.
9. **Labor Hours**: Time spent on the work, in hours.
10. **Parts Cost**: The cost of parts used, entered as a number.
11. **Status**: The current state of the work, such as Complete or Open.
12. **Next Service Due**: The date the asset is next due for service.
13. **Notes**: Readings, observations or follow-up details.

The download includes one sample row followed by blank rows ready for your entries.

## Log vs. work order vs. asset register

A [work order](/learn/work-order) requests and assigns a specific job before it is done. A maintenance log records what was actually done after the work is completed, one entry per event. An [asset register](/solutions/asset-tracking-software) lists the equipment you own and its details, while the log builds up the service history for each asset over time.

## Tips for keeping a useful maintenance log

1. **Use a stable asset ID** — Give every asset one ID and use it consistently in every row, even if the asset's name or location changes. That keeps its full history together when you sort or filter the log.
2. **Add one row per completed event** — Record each job as its own row, whether it was planned or a [corrective repair](/learn/corrective-maintenance). Avoid combining several visits into one line, so labor hours and parts costs stay accurate.
3. **Review the log monthly** — Look for assets with repeated corrective work, rising parts costs or overdue next-service dates. Repeat failures can be fed into an [MTBF calculator](/tools/mtbf-calculator) to see how often an asset breaks down. Move to a [CMMS](/learn/cmms) when the spreadsheet becomes hard to keep accurate and current across your team or sites.

## FAQ

### What is a maintenance log template?

A maintenance log template is a ready-made structure for recording completed maintenance work. This one has 13 columns: date, work order ID, asset ID, asset name, location, maintenance type, description, technician, labor hours, parts cost, status, next service due and notes.

### Can I open the template in Excel or Google Sheets?

Yes. The template is a CSV file, so you can open it in Excel, import it into Google Sheets, or use any spreadsheet app that reads CSV. If you add formatting or formulas, save a copy in your spreadsheet app's own format, because CSV stores plain values only.

### How often should I update a maintenance log?

Add an entry as soon as each job is completed, ideally the same day, while details such as labor hours and parts used are accurate. Review the full log at least monthly to spot recurring issues and upcoming service dates.
