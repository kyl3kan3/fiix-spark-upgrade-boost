# MRO (Maintenance, Repair & Operations)

> The supplies, parts, and consumables a maintenance team uses to keep operations running — separate from raw materials that become finished products.

Canonical URL: https://maintenease.com/learn/mro

## MRO stands for maintenance, repair, and operations

MRO stands for Maintenance, Repair, and Operations — the parts, tools, and consumables used to keep facilities and equipment running, distinct from raw materials that become finished goods. MRO inventory is usually expensed when purchased rather than capitalized like production stock.

## Definition

MRO refers to the goods a business consumes to keep itself running. Spare bearings, lubricants, hand tools, safety gloves, light bulbs, cleaning supplies, and PPE are all MRO. Storerooms often mix critical spares with everyday consumables, which is why disciplined naming, bin locations, and issue records matter as much as the catalog itself.

## MRO supplies list: common items by category

MRO supplies are the items a site buys to keep equipment and buildings working, not to build the product it sells. Typical MRO supplies include fasteners and hardware, bearings and seals, lubricants and greases, filters, belts and hoses, electrical components, cleaning chemicals, PPE, lighting, batteries, and small power tools. The exact list depends on your assets; tie each stocked item to compatible equipment in the CMMS so technicians pick the right part the first time.

## MRO spare parts: critical, insurance, and consumable

MRO spare parts are the replacement components held so equipment can be repaired without waiting on a supplier. A practical way to sort them is by what happens if the part is missing. Critical spares protect assets whose downtime is expensive and whose parts have long lead times, so you hold them even if they rarely move. Insurance spares are a subset: high-cost items kept for a failure that may never happen, justified by the consequence rather than the usage rate. Consumable or fast-moving spares, such as filters, belts, and common bearings, turn over regularly and suit minimum and maximum levels. Everything else can be ordered when a work order calls for it. Record the reasoning with each part so a later cleanup does not scrap the one spare the plant depends on.

## MRO in the supply chain

In supply chain terms, MRO sits between production materials and finished goods: it does not flow into what you sell, but it keeps plants, fleets, and buildings productive. Procurement, receiving, and accounts payable still need PO numbers, lead times, and vendor performance data. Connecting MRO issues to work orders closes the loop — you see which assets consume the most cash and which suppliers miss delivery dates.

## Why MRO is hard to control

MRO inventory can sprawl because items are often low-value individually but material in aggregate. Multiple people may order from different suppliers without checking stock first; critical spares can become hard to find while obsolete parts remain on the shelf. Establish a verified baseline through counts and transaction review instead of assuming a universal inventory-accuracy gap.

## How a CMMS helps

A CMMS may connect on-hand quantity, reorder points, supplier, cost, and asset compatibility to work orders. Products vary: verify whether closing work changes stock automatically, how adjustments are approved, and which low-stock or vendor reports actually exist. The records are useful only when receipts, issues, returns, and counts are entered consistently.

## MRO best practices

Standardize on as few part numbers as possible; consolidate suppliers to negotiate better terms; lock the storeroom and require check-out; do a quarterly cycle count rather than an annual full inventory; and review the slow-moving list every six months to write off true obsolescence.

## FAQ

### What are MRO supplies?

MRO supplies are the parts, consumables, tools, and safety and facility items used to maintain, repair, and operate equipment and buildings. They support production but do not become part of the finished product.

### What are MRO spare parts?

MRO spare parts are replacement components, such as bearings, seals, belts, and motors, kept on hand or ordered to repair equipment. Critical spares are stocked for assets where downtime is costly and lead times are long.

### Is MRO the same as spare parts?

Spare parts are a subset of MRO. MRO also covers consumables, tools, and operating supplies that are not strictly 'parts'.

### How much MRO inventory should I carry?

Enough to cover critical-spare lead times for assets where downtime is expensive, and no more. Anything else ties up cash without reducing risk.
